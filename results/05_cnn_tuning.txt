--------------------------------------------------
Why CNN Tuning?
--------------------------------------------------

The baseline CNN contains three convolutional layers. An additional convolutional layer was introduced to investigate whether increasing the depth of the CNN would allow the model to learn more complex and hierarchical features from the MRI images.

After evaluating the effect of the additional convolutional layer, hyperparameter tuning was performed to investigate whether changing the batch size or learning rate could improve the performance of the deeper CNN.

The tuning experiments were performed by changing one hyperparameter at a time while keeping the other main training settings unchanged.

==================================================
Conclusion
==================================================

The CNN with an additional convolutional layer was first evaluated using a learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 16. This configuration achieved a test accuracy of 75.92% and a macro F1-score of 0.7534.

To investigate whether the performance of the deeper CNN could be improved, hyperparameter tuning was performed by changing the batch size and learning rate independently while keeping the other main training settings unchanged.

Increasing the batch size from 16 to 32 improved the test accuracy from 75.92% to 90.07% and the macro F1-score from 0.7534 to 0.8910. This shows that the larger batch size provided better performance for the deeper CNN under the tested configuration.

The learning rate was then reduced from 0.001 to 0.0001 while keeping the batch size at 16. This configuration achieved a test accuracy of 94.14% and a macro F1-score of 0.9384, which was higher than the other configurations tested in this experiment.

Overall, the results show that the performance of the deeper CNN was strongly affected by the choice of training hyperparameters. The initial configuration with a learning rate of 0.001 and batch size of 16 performed substantially worse, while adjusting the batch size and learning rate resulted in significant improvements.

Among the configurations tested in this experiment, the learning rate of 0.0001 with a batch size of 16 achieved the highest test accuracy of 94.14% and the highest macro F1-score of 0.9384.
