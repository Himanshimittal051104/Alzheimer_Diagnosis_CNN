- Loss Function: CrossEntropyLoss
- Input Size: 224 x 224
- Number of Classes: 4
- Dropout Probability: 0.5

--------------------------------------------------
Why Dropout?
--------------------------------------------------
The baseline CNN achieved very high training accuracy, reaching approximately 99.9%, while the validation accuracy was lower and fluctuated across epochs. This indicates that the model may be overfitting to the training data.

To reduce overfitting, Dropout was introduced into the network. Dropout randomly deactivates a fraction of neurons during training, forcing the model to learn more robust features instead of depending heavily on specific neurons.

In this experiment, Dropout(0.5) was added to the fully connected layers. During training, approximately 50% of the activations are randomly set to zero. During evaluation, Dropout is automatically disabled and all neurons are used.

--------------------------------------------------
Conclusion
--------------------------------------------------

The Dropout model achieved a test accuracy of 96.09% and a macro F1-score of 0.9714.

The model showed consistent improvement during training, with training accuracy increasing from 70.52% in the first epoch to 98.55% in the final epoch. Validation accuracy also improved from 85.11% to 97.73%.

The model achieved strong classification performance across all four classes. The Moderate Impairment class achieved a precision, recall, and F1-score of 1.00. Mild Impairment and No Impairment both achieved an F1-score of 0.97, while Very Mild Impairment achieved an F1-score of 0.95.

Compared with the baseline CNN, the Dropout model achieved slightly lower training accuracy but strong validation and test performance. The results indicate that Dropout helped reduce overfitting while maintaining strong classification performance.

Overall, the experiment demonstrates that adding Dropout with a probability of 0.5 can improve the generalization performance of the CNN for Alzheimer MRI classification.
